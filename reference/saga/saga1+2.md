# Dragon Ball: Resurgence — Saga Outline

## Core Premise

Earth’s Dragon Balls have remained dormant for approximately one hundred years.

Most people no longer believe they ever existed. Those who know the old stories have no explanation for why the Dragon Balls turned to stone or why Shenron disappeared.

Their dormancy was caused by the disappearance of Earth’s Namekian guardian, commonly referred to as **Kami**.

A century ago, Kami confronted a powerful enemy who could not be destroyed through conventional means. Kami used a sealing technique similar to the Mafuba to imprison the enemy within a sealed dimension or vessel.

To ensure the prison held, Kami willingly entered the seal alongside the enemy and reinforced it from within.

While Kami remained trapped and spiritually disconnected from Earth, the Dragon Balls became dormant.

For generations, the seal held.

Then the Dragon Balls suddenly became active again.

Their return was automatic. It occurred because Kami had escaped.

However, Kami’s escape also means that the ancient enemy is no longer being restrained from within.

The seal is failing.

---

# Saga 1: The Dragon Ball Hunt

## Overview

The Dragon Balls awaken after one hundred years of dormancy.

Their return catches nearly everyone by surprise. Ancient stories, forgotten records, and obsolete Dragon Radars suddenly become relevant again.

One organization is prepared.

A heavily armed paramilitary force begins searching for the Dragon Balls almost immediately. They already possess one Dragon Ball when the saga begins and appear to know far more about their return than anyone else.

The organization does not know exactly why the Dragon Balls have awakened. However, its leadership has spent generations waiting for this event.

Saga 1 centers on the race to locate and control the seven Dragon Balls before the paramilitary force can collect them.

---

## The Paramilitary Force

The organization was founded by one of the original sealed villain’s former underlings.

This founder did not remain loyal to their master. They believed the imprisoned villain would eventually escape and feared what would happen when that day came.

However, the founder did not want to prevent the villain’s return.

They wanted to surpass him.

The founder believed the Dragon Balls represented the only realistic means of acquiring enough power to overpower the ancient villain and force him into submission.

Over the following century, that ambition became the foundation of a secret paramilitary organization.

The organization prepared for the eventual return of the Dragon Balls by:

* Preserving information about the original villain.
* Studying ancient accounts of the Dragon Balls.
* Developing or recovering Dragon Ball detection technology.
* Building a military force capable of recovering them.
* Monitoring for signs that Earth’s guardian or the Dragon Balls had returned.
* Preparing a wish intended to grant their leadership overwhelming power.

The organization does not seek to save Earth.

Its leaders believe the ancient villain’s return is inevitable and intend to make him their subordinate before he can reclaim control.

---

## Saga 1 Player Mechanics

### Dragon Ball Searches

Each player may participate in a maximum of **one Dragon Ball search per week**.

A search may include:

* Tracking a Dragon Ball’s signal.
* Exploring the region where it is believed to be located.
* Competing against other searchers.
* Fighting local threats or paramilitary retrieval teams.
* Solving environmental or narrative obstacles.
* Recovering the Dragon Ball from an NPC or protected location.

A successful search does not guarantee that the Dragon Ball will remain secure.

---

### Paramilitary Progress

The paramilitary force begins the saga with **one Dragon Ball already in its possession**.

At the end of each week, the organization secures one remaining unclaimed Dragon Ball.

This creates a campaign-wide countdown. Unless players locate and protect the remaining Dragon Balls, the organization will steadily move closer to collecting all seven.

The organization’s weekly acquisition may be represented through:

* Retrieval operations.
* Military raids.
* Excavations.
* Intelligence obtained from old records.
* Attacks against NPC holders.
* Specialized search teams.

---

### Defending a Dragon Ball

Any player who possesses a Dragon Ball at the end of a week becomes the target of an enemy recovery operation.

A boss-level enemy will attempt to take the Dragon Ball.

The encounter does not always need to be a formal duel. It may take the form of:

* A direct challenge.
* An ambush.
* An attack by an elite retrieval squad.
* An infiltration or theft attempt.
* An assault against the player’s allies or base.
* A coordinated effort to isolate the holder.
* A bargain or threat intended to force surrender.

Dragon Ball holders may recruit allies to help defend them.

Possessing a Dragon Ball should create both narrative importance and immediate risk.

## Saga 1 Structure

### Phase One: The Awakening

The Dragon Balls return.

Players investigate strange signals, resurfacing legends, and reports of orange stones appearing across Earth.

The paramilitary force is introduced through scouts, researchers, soldiers, and retrieval teams.

At this stage, the organization’s exact history and goals remain unclear.

The primary mystery is simple:

**How did they know the Dragon Balls would return?**

---

### Phase Two: The Hunt

The race for the Dragon Balls intensifies.

Players begin recovering Dragon Balls while the paramilitary force launches increasingly aggressive operations.

The organization demonstrates knowledge that should have been lost a century ago.

Players may discover that the organization knew:

* The Dragon Balls would eventually reactivate.
* Their return would signal a major change.
* An ancient threat would follow.
* All seven Dragon Balls would need to be secured quickly.

The organization may not understand the full truth behind the Dragon Balls’ awakening.

Its leaders know enough to be dangerous, but not enough to understand what has actually happened.

---

### Phase Three: The Collection War

Once most of the Dragon Balls have been located, the saga shifts away from simple searches.

The conflict becomes a war over possession.

Possible events include:

* Raids against paramilitary facilities.
* Attempts to steal captured Dragon Balls.
* Convoy attacks.
* Prisoner rescues.
* Defenses of player-controlled locations.
* Battles against elite commanders.
* Intelligence operations targeting the organization’s leadership.
* A final attempt by the organization to assemble all seven Dragon Balls.

The paramilitary force begins consolidating its remaining resources and preparing to make its wish.

---

### Finale: Defeat of the Paramilitary Force

The players defeat the organization and prevent its leadership from using the Dragon Balls to gain control over the ancient villain.

The organization’s leadership may reveal that it never believed the Dragon Balls’ return was a miracle.

Its founders viewed their awakening as a warning.

The final confrontation should resolve the immediate Dragon Ball conflict while leaving the larger mystery intact.

Possible final warning:

> “You believe their awakening means the danger has passed. It means the one who created them has returned. Ask yourselves what he was running from.”

Alternatively:

> “We spent a century waiting for the Dragon Balls to awaken. Not because we wanted their return, but because we knew what would follow.”

The paramilitary group is defeated.

The Dragon Balls are secured.

The reason for their return remains unknown to the players.

---

# Interlude: Kami’s Return

Kami escaped from the seal shortly before the beginning of Saga 1.

His escape automatically restored the Dragon Balls because their existence is tied to his life and spiritual connection to Earth.

Kami did not leave the seal because he abandoned his duty.

He escaped because remaining inside had become more dangerous than leaving.

Over the course of a century, the imprisoned villain learned to siphon energy from Kami.

Kami’s presence originally reinforced the prison. As his strength declined, the villain began using that same connection to drain divine energy and weaken him further.

If Kami had remained:

* The villain would eventually have absorbed him.
* The seal would still have broken.
* The villain would have escaped with far greater power.
* The villain might have gained access to Kami’s divine abilities.
* The Dragon Balls themselves may have become vulnerable to the villain’s influence.

Kami therefore made a calculated decision.

By escaping, he weakened the prison’s internal reinforcement and accelerated its collapse. However, he also denied the villain the chance to absorb the remainder of his power.

Kami chose an earlier, weaker escape over a later, catastrophic one.

The effort left him severely drained.

After returning to Earth, Kami is unable to leave his tower. He lacks the strength to communicate with anyone below and must spend Saga 1 recovering.

By the beginning of Saga 2, he has regained enough power to contact the players.

# Saga 2: The Broken Seal

## Overview

Kami finally communicates with the players and reveals the truth behind the Dragon Balls’ dormancy.

A century ago, he sealed away a powerful enemy and entered the prison voluntarily to reinforce it.

His escape restored the Dragon Balls.

It also left the ancient enemy restrained only by the failing outer seal.

The paramilitary force from Saga 1 represented the legacy of a traitorous underling.

Saga 2 introduces the descendants, followers, or surviving servants who remained loyal to the imprisoned villain.

These loyalists have spent generations preparing for the moment when their master could finally be freed.

The return of the Dragon Balls confirms that Kami has escaped and that the outer seal can now be affected from Earth.

Saga 2 centers on the struggle to prevent the loyalists from completing their master’s release.

---

## The Loyalist Force

The loyalists differ from the paramilitary organization in several important ways.

The paramilitary force wanted to overpower and enslave the ancient villain.

The loyalists worship, serve, or remain devoted to him.

Their purpose is to restore their master and return him to power.

Over the last century, they have:

* Preserved their master’s history and teachings.
* Searched for the outer seal and its anchors.
* Recovered objects connected to their master.
* Infiltrated locations associated with Kami or the original battle.
* Prepared rituals and technology capable of weakening the prison.
* Maintained hidden bases, bloodlines, or succession systems.
* Waited for proof that Kami was no longer reinforcing the seal.

They could not free their master earlier because Kami was maintaining the prison from within.

Any attack against the outer seal would have been counteracted by Kami’s presence.

Once Kami escaped, the inner reinforcement disappeared.

The Dragon Balls’ awakening served as the loyalists’ signal that the time had come.

---

## Structure of the Seal

The prison consists of two primary layers.

### The Inner Restraint

Kami’s presence within the prison actively suppressed the ancient villain.

This restraint no longer exists.

A portion of Kami’s energy may still remain trapped inside, slowing the villain’s escape and preventing him from immediately returning at full strength.

---

### The Outer Seal

The outer seal is connected to several physical or spiritual anchors located across Earth.

These anchors may take the form of:

* Ancient shrines.
* Buried monuments.
* Guardian relics.
* Sealed temples.
* Magical formations.
* Locations connected to the original battle.
* Objects containing portions of the sealing technique.

As long as the anchors remain intact, the ancient villain cannot fully escape.

The loyalists must weaken or destroy enough of these anchors to create a stable opening.

---

## Loyalist Objectives

The loyalists may weaken the prison through several coordinated methods.

### Destroying Seal Anchors

Each damaged anchor reduces the stability of the prison.

Destroying an anchor may allow more of the villain’s power and influence to reach Earth.

---

### Recovering a Sympathetic Relic

The loyalists seek an object closely connected to their master.

Possible relics include:

* A weapon.
* A piece of armor.
* A fragment of the villain’s body.
* An object infused with his ki.
* A personal seal or emblem.
* An item used during the original imprisonment.

The relic can be used to establish a connection between Earth and the sealed dimension.

---

### Feeding Energy Into the Prison

Once a connection is established, the loyalists can begin transferring energy to their master.

This energy may be collected through:

* Power-draining technology.
* Captured fighters.
* Stolen energy reserves.
* Ritual sites.
* Corrupted seal anchors.
* Energy gathered from populated regions.
* Ancient devices created for the villain’s return.

The loyalists must deliberately collect and channel this energy. Ordinary battles do not automatically strengthen the imprisoned villain.

---

### Reaching Kami

Kami represents both a threat and a source of power.

The loyalists may attempt to:

* Kill him.
* Capture him.
* Drain his remaining energy.
* Force him to reveal the location of the seal.
* Use his connection to the prison to stabilize the release.
* Prevent him from repairing damaged anchors.

An attack against Kami’s tower may become a major event during Saga 2.

---

# Saga 2 Arc Structure

## Arc One: The Guardian’s Warning

Kami regains enough strength to contact the players.

He reveals:

* He is the creator of Earth’s Dragon Balls.
* His imprisonment caused their dormancy.
* His escape caused their automatic return.
* The ancient enemy remains imprisoned.
* The outer seal is deteriorating.
* Loyal followers are moving to free their master.
* Kami cannot personally leave his tower or confront them.

The players must locate the seal anchors before the loyalists can destroy them.

This arc focuses on:

* Learning the truth about the past.
* Investigating ancient locations.
* Defending seal anchors.
* Fighting loyalist scouts and commanders.
* Recovering records or relics.
* Establishing the loyalists as a distinct threat.

By the end of the arc, at least one anchor is damaged.

This damage allows the imprisoned villain to communicate with Earth for the first time.

He may speak through:

* Dreams.
* Telepathic messages.
* Corrupted fighters.
* Damaged seal sites.
* Temporary projections.
* The recovered relic.

The enemy is still trapped, but he is no longer silent.

---

## Arc Two: The Breaking Seal

The loyalists escalate into open conflict.

They launch simultaneous operations across Earth.

Possible objectives include:

* Destroying the remaining anchors.
* Recovering their master’s relic.
* Attacking Kami’s tower.
* Capturing powerful fighters as energy sources.
* Activating a device capable of opening the prison.
* Corrupting a repaired anchor.
* Protecting the ritual site where the release will occur.

The players cannot stop every operation at once.

Their choices determine:

* Which anchors survive.
* Which loyalist commanders remain active.
* Whether the relic is recovered.
* Whether Kami remains safe.
* How much energy reaches the villain.
* How stable the final opening becomes.

As the seal weakens, the villain gains increasing influence.

### Stage One

The villain can communicate with his followers.

### Stage Two

The villain can project visions, exert mental pressure, and influence weakened targets.

### Stage Three

The villain can manifest temporary energy projections or partial avatars.

### Stage Four

The villain can directly assist his followers and begin forcing the prison open.

At the climax of Arc Two, the loyalists attempt a complete release.

The players interfere and prevent the intended ritual from being completed perfectly.

Realizing that his followers may fail, the villain forces his way through the damaged seal prematurely.

The seal breaks.

The villain escapes.

However, the circumstances of his release depend on player performance.

---

## Arc Three: The Unbound Enemy

The villain is physically free but has not necessarily returned at full strength.

A large portion of his original power may remain:

* Trapped within the ruined prison.
* Bound to the surviving anchors.
* Contained inside his relic.
* Entangled with Kami’s remaining energy.
* Scattered across locations damaged during his escape.

The villain’s immediate objective is to restore himself.

Possible restoration goals include:

* Absorbing the remaining energy inside the prison.
* Reclaiming his ancient relic.
* Destroying and consuming the surviving seal anchors.
* Capturing Kami.
* Reabsorbing a separated portion of his essence.
* Reaching the site where his original power was sealed.
* Draining energy from Earth on a massive scale.

The surviving loyalists attempt to protect their master and buy him time.

The players must pursue the villain, defeat the remaining loyalist leadership, and prevent his complete restoration.

Saga 2 ends with the final confrontation against the ancient enemy.

The saga does not end merely because he escapes.

His escape concludes Arc Two.

His defeat concludes Saga 2.

---

# Variable Escape Outcomes

The seal will ultimately break.

The players cannot preserve a failing prison forever.

However, their actions determine the conditions under which the villain escapes.

The players are not deciding whether the villain returns.

They are deciding how strong he is, what resources he possesses, and what condition Earth is in when he emerges.

---

## Strong Player Success

If the players protect most objectives:

* The villain escapes severely weakened.
* Most loyalist commanders are defeated.
* The villain’s relic remains outside his control.
* Several seal anchors remain intact.
* Kami survives and can provide limited support.
* The villain is forced to retreat after escaping.
* The players begin Arc Three with a major strategic advantage.

---

## Mixed Outcome

If the players win some objectives and lose others:

* The villain escapes with moderate strength.
* One or more loyalist commanders survive.
* The villain controls either his relic or part of the seal’s power.
* One major location is damaged during the escape.
* Kami remains alive but may be injured or further weakened.
* Both sides enter Arc Three with meaningful advantages.

---

## Poor Player Outcome

If the loyalists complete most of their objectives:

* The villain escapes close to full strength.
* Most of his loyalist commanders remain active.
* The villain recovers his relic.
* The outer seal is completely destroyed.
* Kami may be captured or drained.
* A major region is devastated during the release.
* The villain begins Arc Three from a position of dominance.

---

# Saga Themes

## Saga 1

**Ambition, control, and inherited betrayal.**

The paramilitary force believes power is the only protection against a greater evil.

Its leaders do not want to save Earth from the ancient villain.

They want to become powerful enough to command him.

---

## Saga 2

**Duty, sacrifice, and the consequences of containment.**

Kami sacrificed a century of his existence to hold the ancient villain.

His escape was necessary, but it placed Earth in immediate danger.

The players inherit a conflict that began long before they were born and must finish what Kami could only delay.

---

# Central Reveal

The Dragon Balls’ awakening was never the true crisis.

It was the first visible sign that a century-old prison had begun to fail.

The people of Earth saw the Dragon Balls return and believed a miracle had occurred.

In reality, the lock had opened.

And something was still trapped behind the door.
