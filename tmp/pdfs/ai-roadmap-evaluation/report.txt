AI Engine Roadmap Evaluation for Dragonball
Legacy
Executive summary
The proposed @dragonball-resurgence/ai-engine roadmap is architecturally strong and, with a
few contract corrections, is a good implementation plan for this repository. Its most important design
choice is correct: the AI is a policy layer over combat-engine legal decisions, not another combat runtime.
That matches the repository's existing architecture, which assigns legality, rolls, damage, resources,
statuses, transformations, counters, completion, and state transitions exclusively to combat-engine ,
while the planned AI package scores only engine-provided choices. 
There is currently no packages/ai-engine package on master , and the packages directory contains
only combat-engine , game-config , game-data , npc-ai , persistence , shared , and 
transformation-evaluator . The architecture directory likewise contains 
combat-ai-simulation.md and the combat-engine architecture/progress/roadmap/matrix documents,
but no ai-engine-progress.md or AI capability-matrix document. npc-ai remains effectively empty:
its public source is just export {}; . 
The combat engine, however, is far more AI-ready than the older combat-ai-simulation.md status
text implies. Current combat state includes schema/version information, rules version, combatants, active
effects, action history, serialized resolution frames, scheduled work, pending decisions, and explicit
completed states. The public contract includes LegalDecision discriminants for pass, power-up,
surrender, basic attack, move use, transformation activation/deactivation, item use, and pending-decision
responses. The August 29 progress record shows Phase 9 transformation/race closure complete and
identifies Phase 10 scope-boundary classification as the next combat-engine resume point.

Three changes should be made before treating the roadmap as implementation-ready.
First, resolve the pending-decision contract. CombatDecisionInput now supports 
selectedOptionIds for normalized multi-selection, and the combat progress record explicitly
documents one , all , and up-to pending-selection behavior. Yet LegalDecision still represents a
pending answer as exactly one optionId . The AI roadmap's invariant that its result is “exactly one
decision from the supplied legal set” therefore needs either a richer engine legal-decision representation
for complete selections or a documented engine-owned method for materializing a complete pending
choice.
Second, add an explicit AI analysis boundary to the design. AI-110 currently implies that the AI will infer
evaluation features by combining LegalDecision , FightState , and raw declarative game-data .
That is safe for identities and plainly declarative metadata, but it becomes dangerous when the AI starts
deciding what an effect means. The repository already has a compiled/executor combat architecture
specifically to prevent different consumers from independently interpreting mechanics. The AI should
therefore consume either combat-engine-authored decision descriptors or public combat-engine analysis/
probe functions for mechanically significant facts.
Third, treat deterministic lookahead randomness as a distinct design problem from ordinary AI noise. The
current SeededRandomSource is deterministic but stateful, exposes only integer() , and has no clone/
fork operation. Combat dependencies also include clock and generated IDs. That is sufficient for combat
replay, but not by itself sufficient for order-independent speculative branches. Phase 7 should use branch-
local deterministic combat dependencies derived from an analysis seed and branch identity, never the live
fight's RNG object.

Gaps in the roadmap itself
The roadmap is unusually comprehensive, but I would make five substantive adjustments.
Explicitly include surrender in immediate terminal utility. surrender is a real LegalDecision , but
Phase 2's baseline list names attacks, power-up, pass, defense and item use without explicitly naming
surrender. It does not need its own phase, but AI-220 should be described as damage, survival, KO, and
terminal-outcome utility. Otherwise a generic baseline score of zero could accidentally make voluntary
defeat competitive with an unattractive but viable action.
Treat transformation deactivation as explicitly part of AI-320. Both activation and deactivation are legal
decision discriminants. The transformation evaluator should therefore handle not only “is transforming
worth it?” but also “is remaining transformed worth it?” against costs, cooldowns, HP/state effects and
current tactical horizon.
Resolve multi-selection before AI-030 is declared complete. A fallback that “supports pending decisions”
is not actually complete if the engine can require/select multiple candidates but the legal-decision
representation only exposes one option at a time. This is the roadmap's most concrete contract issue.
Move structured diagnostics scaffolding earlier. AI-800–840 can remain the full diagnostics phase, but the 
ScoreFactor , candidate identity, evaluator version and provenance model should exist by AI-200.
Retrofitting provenance after six phases of evaluators will be unnecessarily painful.
Add a combat-analysis/probe checkpoint before AI-600/700. Outcome probabilities and lookahead are
exactly where a well-behaved evaluator can accidentally turn into a second combat engine. The roadmap
repeatedly says not to do so; the cleanest enforcement is a formal interface through which AI asks combat-
engine to describe or execute mechanics rather than reconstructing them.
Recommended implementation sequence
I would preserve almost all of your phase order, but insert a contract-hardening milestone before Phase
0 and bring the minimal diagnostic schema forward.
Priority and effort plan
Effort here is relative implementation effort, not elapsed-time promises.

I would not collapse this into a conventional minimax/search project. Your roadmap is right to build a
strong static evaluator before search. The combat state is already rich enough that a good immediate/
contextual evaluator should solve a substantial portion of “obvious intelligence” cheaply, leaving expensive
branching for genuinely ambiguous candidates. That is especially important for a future simulator, where
every extra engine probe will be multiplied across many fights. The repository's AI/simulation architecture
already describes this bounded static-evaluation → pruning → shallow-lookahead pipeline and explicitly
rejects initial full MCTS. 
Technical design
Public AI API
I would slightly refine the conceptual API from combat-ai-simulation.md . The document currently
proposes state, actor, legal decisions, game data, personality, difficulty and RNG as inputs.

letting AI code silently use global registries. The future simulation architecture explicitly wants immutable
simulation-only data variants and must avoid mutation of live registries. An injected view makes that future
use case much easier.

This should be produced from combat-engine-owned compiled/authoritative information, not
recreated by the AI from source prose.
Why? The repository has invested heavily in exactly this separation. Game definitions are declarative; the
combat engine compiles and executes typed effects; source/display prose must not become executable
authority. Current combat progress also records migrations specifically removing runtime prose
interpretation and replacing it with typed rule tokens and selectors. 
The AI can still infer strategy. For example:
combat descriptor says:
  applies status X for 1 target turn
  prevents attack category Y
  costs 4 Ki
AI says:
  opponent currently has 4 strong Y actions
  therefore denial-value = +11
  one-turn horizon discount = 0.65
  Ki-cost = -5
That is AI reasoning.
This would be wrong:
AI reads move prose
  -> recognizes "cannot use Advanced Attacks"
  -> implements its own interpretation of the lock
That is a second rules engine.
Deterministic randomness
The combat engine currently defines a minimal RandomSource.integer() API, plus deterministic 
SeededRandomSource . Its state is private and advances with each request. 
Do not simply give one SeededRandomSource to AI evaluation and call it sequentially while iterating
candidates. Doing so makes this change dangerous:
20


=== PAGE 21 ===
// Candidate C is added by a future game-data update.
[A, B] -> [A, B, C]
If random calls are sequential, the noise consumed for B may change simply because C was inserted or
because sort/evaluation order changed.
Prefer stable derived streams:
interface AiRandomSource {
readonly seed: number;
integer(
key: string,
minimum: number,
maximum: number,
): number;
chance(
key: string,
probability: number,
): boolean;
}
Conceptually:
noise("decision:use-move:foo")
tieBreak("rank:use-move:foo")
mistake("decision-root")
Each result should derive from:
root AI seed
+ evaluator/profile version
+ stable purpose
+ canonical decision key
rather than from “how many calls happened before this one.”
This buys you an extremely useful property:
Reordering the supplied legal decisions does not change scores or the winner when all
semantic inputs are otherwise equal.

AI randomness must also remain distinct from combat randomness. Your roadmap already states this

Lookahead sandboxing
The cleanest lookahead implementation is not to clone the live combat RNG.
Use:
interface CombatAnalysisProbe {
enumerateLegalDecisions(
state: FightState,
actorId: CombatantId,
): readonly LegalDecision[];
transition(
state: FightState,
decision: LegalDecision,
branch: AnalysisBranch,
): CombatAnalysisTransition;
}
Internally, each AnalysisBranch gets its own:
branch combat seed
fixed clock
branch-local deterministic ID source
work/node budget
derived from a stable path such as:
root analysis seed
  / candidate key
  / outcome branch
  / response key
  / depth
The speculative branch must never receive the production fight's CombatDependencies object.

The repository already has deterministic test implementations for clock, random sequences and generated

Likewise, avoid structuredClone(state) by default. The engine's stated transition contract is non-
mutating, and state members are exposed as readonly structures. If public transitions genuinely preserve
input state, lookahead can safely pass the same snapshot as input and retain returned successor states.
Add deepFreeze /mutation-sentinel tests to prove this. Only add a clone helper if profiling demonstrates
that some integration actually needs one.

One subtle refinement: your Phase 7 safeguard says “time or work budgets where appropriate.” Never use
a wall-clock timeout as the selection-determining budget in deterministic simulation mode. A 10 ms
cutoff can expand a different number of branches under different machines/load and therefore change the
selected action. Use deterministic node/probe/work limits for reproducible profiles. Wall-clock cancellation
may still abort external work, but should not silently become part of deterministic strategy.

That boundary is the strongest aspect of the roadmap, and it fits the repository unusually well. combat-
engine already has immutable/versioned state, structured pending work, deterministic dependencies,
typed legal decisions, calculation tracing, scheduling, item support, transformations, and a capability-
accounting culture. The AI roadmap should reuse those patterns rather than invent a different
architecture.
The one thing I would not do is begin by teaching the AI individual Dragon Ball moves. The roadmap is
absolutely right to reject that workflow. Build the evaluator around reusable concepts—terminal value,
damage, resource pressure, scarcity, option denial, lifecycle windows, action economy and engine-driven
futures—and make the capability matrix prove what those concepts cover. That gives you an AI engine that
can grow with the game's declarative catalog instead of accumulating a second, increasingly impossible-to-
maintain collection of move rules.
